* Survivors, each argued equivalent (nothing observable changes):
  1. `eval.rs:379` `tick`: `now > clock` -> `>=`; `advance_clock` returns at once when `to <= clock`.
  2. `exec.rs:241` `close_qty > 0 &&` -> `>=`: with `close_qty == 0` the remainder is the whole order, whose quantity was validated at arrival with the same market configuration.
  3. `exec.rs:269` and 4. `exec.rs:270` `it.sl_e12 != 0` / `it.tp_e12 != 0` -> `==`: the levels only fill `pos_after`, which feeds the margin check; the check uses `p.p` (quantity, entry), never the levels.
  5. `exec.rs:292` `close_qty > 0 && open_qty == 0` -> `>=`: `close_qty == 0` implies `open_qty == it.qty != 0`.
  6. `exec.rs:310` `close_qty > 0` -> `<` (the `after_flip` argument): after a flip's closing event the position is gone, so `positions.get` is `None` either way.
  7. `exec.rs:463` `over || would_over` -> `&&`: `would_over` (the slice alone exceeds the limit) implies `over` (after the other closes the tally only grew); without `would_over` nothing else was closed, so `over == would_over`.
  8. `pass.rs:33` `||` -> `&&` inside `failed`: `B <= F_s` while Active and flat has already become a `Fail` event (status Failed), so only the first operand can be true at that point.
  9. to 11. `sha256.rs:51` and `:54` (two) `^` -> `|`: in `ch` the operands are disjoint masks (`e & f` and `!e & g`), and `(a&b) | ((a&c) ^ (b&c))` is the majority function again (checked for all eight bit combinations).
  12. `sha256.rs:78` `filled > 0` -> `>=`: with `filled == 0` the branch copies the same bytes into the block and compresses when full, which the generic path does too.

## Public API (one line per item)
* `Evaluator::new(RulesCfg, AccountParams, Box<dyn IntentVerifier>) -> Result<Evaluator, ConfigError>`: a fresh account (B = S, hash chain at its genesis, archive with its first header, intents file with its header line).
* `Evaluator::on_sample(market, ts_ns, value_e18, recv_ns, digest) -> Result<(), SampleError>` and `on_sample_mid(market, sec, mid_e12)`: records a price update (non-decreasing source second across all markets).
* `Evaluator::on_order(&SignedIntent) -> Result<(), OrderReject>`: a trader's signed order (signature through the injected `IntentVerifier`); an accepted order is written to the intents file; it executes when its second closes.
* `Evaluator::tick(now)`: backend time moved to second `now`; every earlier second is closed and processed (rollover fees, floors, triggers, pending orders, queued orders).
* `Evaluator::drain_batches(&BatchPolicy) -> Vec<Batch>`: the queue as batches (`JournalBatch` + markets + `Meta` commitments + the account as it must read afterwards + modelled transaction size and compute units).
* `Evaluator::request_pass(now, &ChainView, ReplayCheck) -> PassDecision`: `Confirm` or `Reject(NotFlat | JournalBehind | TargetNotReached | Other)` (the `reject_pass` codes); freezes trading.
* `Evaluator::phase_started(phase)`, `pass_resolved()`, `set_held(bool)`, `end_externally()`: lifecycle edges the chain drives.
* Accessors: `state()`, `positions()`, `pending()`, `status()`, `trades_closed()`, `seq()`, `head()`, `stats()`, `refusals()`, `archive_bytes()`, `sample_stream()`, `sample(i)`, `intents_bytes()`, `quick_headroom()`, `clock()`, `processed_through()`, `queued_events()`, `fault()`, `is_ended()`, `is_frozen()`.

## The model
* **Time.** Whole seconds. A second is *closed* when the evaluator's clock (newest second of any sample, order or `tick`) passes it; a closed second is processed once, in this order: rollover holding fees; the decision pass over its samples; the events that follow (tight start / raise, stop-loss and take-profit closes, pending fills, then the breach or fail tail); the second's queued orders; `SetSlTp` last.
* **Decisions** use the account as it stood at the START of the second (after the rollover fees) and the latest sample of every market as marks, sample by sample; of several samples of one second giving the same kind of decision, the last one's numbers count (handover/verifier-formats.md 7). This is exactly the verifier's walk; the evaluator calls the same engine functions (`evaluate_sample`, `check_protective`, `check_pending`) and, before it writes a claim, runs the program's own check (`check_close`, `check_tight_start`, `check_floor_raised`, `check_breach`, `check_tight_end`) on it.
* **Orders.** Arrival checks (signature, nonce, decoding, stale/future time, quantity, market, levels, pending limit, fresh price, session) leave nothing behind when they fail. An accepted order is written to the intents file and executes when its second closes at the newest sample of its market that existed when it arrived; margin, locks, holds, caps and a market that closed meanwhile can still refuse it then (`refusals()`), leaving only the intents line.
* **Rollover fees.** One `Fee{Holding}` per position and night at `R = 22:00:00 UTC`, dated `R`, mark = bid (long) / ask (short) of the last sample of the market with second <= R, rate 0 = no event. One night's fees are one unit (chunks of seven).
* **Commission.** `Fee{Commission}` after every `Fill` and `Close`, `ceil(rate x margin_micro(qty, price, 1))`; the closing commission estimate is part of `E`.
* **Pass.** After `request_advance` the account is frozen; `request_pass` answers from its own state (queued events or another head: `JournalBehind`; a position or a chain that is not flat: `NotFlat`; pending orders, another balance or phase, a failed phase or a watchdog mismatch: `Other`; `B < 1.08 S`: `TargetNotReached`).

## Batching and the SPLIT rule (`drain_batches`)
* The queue holds **units**: events that must stay together: a flip (`Close, Fee, Fill, Fee` under one intent: the verifier sums quantities per intent per batch), the last close with its `Fee` and the `Breach`, `TightEnd` or `Fail` that follows it, one night's holding fees (so the verifier never judges second R between two of them), a close with its commission.
* A batch takes units in order while: events including the `Meta` <= 8; distinct proofs <= 3; distinct market accounts <= 8; modelled signed transaction <= 1,232 bytes; modelled compute <= 200,000 CU; and, if it holds a `TightEnd`, `Breach` or `Fail`, it ends flat (`Meta.flat`, program I-9/I-10/I-11). A unit that does not fit alone is emitted alone. A `Fail` ends its batch, which is a `report_fail` batch (`..., Fail, Meta`).
* **Transaction size** is exact (checked against the serialized transaction of every batch of the gate run): `1 + 64 + 3 + shortvec(keys) + 32 x keys + 32 + 1 + (1 + shortvec(n_acc) + n_acc + shortvec(data_len) + data_len)`; `post_journal` names 6 fixed accounts + the markets, `report_fail` 11 + the markets; the transaction has ONE signature (the evaluator is the fee payer); a `SetComputeUnitLimit` instruction would add 32 + 8 bytes. **Compute** is a bound `30,000 + 12,500 x events + 8,000 x proofs (+ 40,000 for report_fail)`; every batch of the gate run stayed under it and under 200,000.
* **Meta.** `time` = `min(processed_through, time of the next queued event)`, and one second less than the next event when that is a holding fee (the verifier judges the samples up to `Meta.time` with the account as the journal left it, so the fee dated R must precede a Meta dated R). `samples_hash` = sha256 of the first n records of the stream, n = samples with source second <= `Meta.time` (a prefix because the stream is in second order); `intents_hash` = sha256 of the whole intents file so far. Both are never shorter than before.
* Different policies give different `Meta` placement, hence different chains and sequence numbers, but the same account state, book, archive, intents and event content: property-tested (`tests/props.rs`, which says so).

## Findings: disagreements between the DESIGN, the program and the verifier
None of engine, journal-types, verifier or program was changed. Each finding is a deterministic test that ASSERTS THE DISAGREEMENT (`crates/evaluator/tests/findings.rs`, `tests-rs/tests/trust_matrix.rs`, `finding_*`); when a component is fixed the test fails and says so. The generator (`testkit::generate`) is built to stay clear of findings 1 and 2; `generate_wild` (ENGINE_WILD=1) is the unguarded exploration mode: 100,000 wild paths found only findings 1 and 2 again (7 + 1 failures), nothing new.

1. **A pending order that only reduces has no `Fill`.** DESIGN 7.2 N-9 nets a filled pending order normally; when it only reduces a position the journal has a `Close` and no `Fill`, and the verifier's walk matches triggered pending orders only against `Fill` events of kind Limit/Stop: honest history, `TriggerMissed`. Minimal: `finding_1_...` (long 1M EUR, sell limit 250k above the market). Needs: the verifier should accept a `Close` whose `intent_id` is the pending order's nonce, or the design should say a pending order may not reduce. The generator keeps one side of pending orders per market (secondary markets only) so none can reduce.
2. **Commission versus the quick-limit fail.** DESIGN 4.4 I-8 allows nothing after a quick overshoot but the `Fail`; module 9 (convention 4) wants a commission `Fee` after every `Close`. With a non-zero commission both cannot hold: the program refuses `[Close, Fee, Fail]` (`QuickLimitExceeded` 6018: `tests-rs finding_2`), so the evaluator omits that fee and the verifier says `FeeMismatch`. Variant: a flip whose closing slice overshoots drops its remainder, so the verifier also says `ForgedTrade` ("trades under intent N do not add up"). Needs: allow commission `Fee` events between the overshoot and the `Fail` in the program, or drop the fee of that slice in the verifier. The generator uses commission 0 on quick-limit and noise histories.
3. **Margin is checked when a pending order fills, but the verifier expects every triggered order to fill.** DESIGN 7.3 / decision 27. An order refused for margin stays pending and writes nothing (evaluator), the verifier says `TriggerMissed` (`finding_3_...`). The generator keeps committed margin under 45% of S so no pending fill is ever refused (the campaigns assert `refused_fills == 0`).
4. **A breach whose forced closes leave B at or below the static floor.** A profitable quick slice is credited only half, so B after the closes can be below the equity that triggered the breach, even below `F_s`. The program refuses the `Breach` (`BreachBelowStaticFloor` 6077, `tests-rs finding_4`) and wants `report_fail`; the evaluator posts `Fail(StaticFloor)`; the verifier's walk expects a Breach at that sample: `MissedBreach` (`finding_4_...`, two markets, B = 92,500). Second form: the forced closes push the quick tally over its limit, so the breach is posted as `Fail(PhaseQuick)` and the verifier again says `MissedBreach` (`finding_4_a_breach_whose_closes_overshoot_the_quick_tally`). The flip variant of finding 2 is pinned in `exec_edges::a_flip_whose_closing_slice_overshoots_the_quick_limit_fails_the_phase`. Needs: the verifier (or the design) to treat "breach whose closes end at or below F_s" as a fail.
5. **`PassFinalized` and `PhaseAdvanced` both start the next phase.** The program emits both for one `finalize_pass` (the second one marks the phase start); the verifier's log format has a line for each and each applies a phase change, so an indexer that mirrors both gets `StateMismatch` (`tests-rs finding_5`). The harness writes only `pass_finalized` (and `phase_advanced` for the phase-1 confirmation). Needs: say so in handover/verifier-formats.md, or make the verifier ignore a `phase_advanced` that follows a `pass_finalized`.

Smaller points, all consistent with the design but worth the owner's eye:
* **Decision 27 / DESIGN 16 ("orders deleted on breach lock and phase change") cannot be done by the evaluator alone**: deleting a pending order needs a trader-signed cancel, otherwise the verifier keeps expecting it after the lock. The evaluator keeps pending orders (blocked while locked) and answers a pass request with `reject_pass(Other)` while any is left. Decision 52.
* **The verifier does not judge a `Fill` against `lock_until`** (trust matrix: PROGRAM only), nor sessions or status at order time (module 9, decision 12).
* **A forced `Close` (breach, fail) needs a proof no older than the market's `max_age_secs`.** A closed or quiet market must therefore keep publishing (frozen) samples; if the feed stops, an honest breach is unpostable. The campaigns publish frozen samples for closed markets. DESIGN 3.4 does not say this.
* **Several samples of different kinds in one second can ask for conflicting actions** (a stop-loss and a take-profit of one position from two samples; a pending fill after a breach in the same second): the evaluator acts on the first, the verifier reports the other as `TriggerMissed`. 5% of generated histories put two markets' samples in one second and stay clean; the conflict needs two samples of one market straddling both levels within a second.
* **A fee dated R followed by a `Meta` dated R.** See the Meta rule above. With more than seven positions one night's fees are chunked 7 + 1 and the second chunk's batch can still be cut after the verifier has judged second R; harmless unless a sample sits exactly at R and a decision depends on the last fee.
* **The 12 h lock is checked against event time, `terminal_at` against block time.** Nothing to do.

## DECISIONS (design silent; smallest safe option)
1. Orders execute when their second closes (latency up to one second of backend time); a sample or order dated before the evaluator's clock is refused (`OutOfOrder`, `StaleOrder`); an order more than 5 s ahead is refused (`FutureOrder`).
2. Orders in a closed or quiet market (outside the sessions, or no price change within `max_age_secs`, or the newest sample older than `max_age_secs`) are refused, closes included; forced closes use the latest sample whatever its age.
3. Margin: after the order, `b' + P' - closing-commission estimate - this fill's commission >= sum of margins` (margin of a position uses its average entry); pure reduces skip it.
4. A triggered pending order that cannot fill stays pending; pending orders live through locks and phases (finding notes); at most 10 pending and 8 positions (A-CAP-1).
5. SL/TP of a pending order are validated at arrival against its own level (not at fill); a market order's against the fill sample; a gap past them is the trader's.
6. `SetSlTp` applies at the end of its second to a position opened at or before its `client_time` (the walker's rule); zero clears.
7. Quick-limit order of events: when a slice would push `Q` over the limit, every other position (and the rest of this one) is closed first (reason Fail, intent 0), then the slice, then `Fail{PhaseQuick}`, no commission fee on that slice (finding 2). Forced closes are ordered by the quick tally they add, smallest first.
8. A breach whose closes leave `B <= F_s` or `Q` over the limit is posted as the matching `Fail` (finding 4); a breach whose closes leave `B` above `L` (another second's gain) just ends the mode (`TightEnd`).
9. `Meta.time`, the proof table (first-use order), the segment per UTC day in the archive, the `Meta` commitments, and the flat-ending rule are as in "Batching".
10. `Stats`, `refusals()` and `testkit` are test support; they never influence a decision.
11. Pass scenarios: `request_pass` does not drain; the caller posts first (queued events mean `JournalBehind`).
12. The campaigns' harness writes `pass_requested`, `pass_confirmed`, `phase_advanced` (phase 1) and `pass_finalized` lines from the program's own events (finding 5).
13. No heartbeat `Meta` is built after `end_externally()` (an account the chain has graduated accepts no more batches).
